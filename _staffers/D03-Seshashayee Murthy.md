description: |
  We are evaluating student mastery from time-series data generated through interactions with intelligent tutoring systems. The goal is to estimate how a student’s understanding of individual concepts changes over time and predict whether the student can apply those concepts independently. Relevant evidence includes diagnostic assessments, sequences of problem attempts, answer correctness, response times, requests for hints, use of AI assistance, and performance on subsequent unassisted assessments. A central challenge is distinguishing successful task completion with assistance from durable learning that transfers to new problems. Models must account for differences in question difficulty, questions involving multiple concepts, dependencies among concepts, and the effects of practice, spacing, and forgetting. Approaches include Bayesian knowledge tracing, hidden Markov models, and other sequential estimation methods that update mastery estimates as new evidence arrives. These estimates should be validated against future unassisted performance, including proctored assessments, and should support instructional decisions such as identifying knowledge gaps, selecting practice activities, and adjusting the level of scaffolding.
