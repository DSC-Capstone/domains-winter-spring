description: |
  Radar-based sensing offers a promising approach to understanding occupancy and activity in physical environments without relying on cameras or personally identifiable visual information. However, translating raw radar measurements into reliable occupancy information presents significant data science challenges, particularly when environmental conditions, sensor placement, interference, and signal characteristics vary. This domain explores how radar data can be processed, analyzed, and evaluated to detect the presence or absence of objects in real-world environments, with smart parking as an initial application. Students will investigate signal preprocessing, feature extraction, classification, uncertainty estimation, and robustness under changing conditions. They will explore how models trained in one environment perform in another, identify sources of false detections, and evaluate approaches using reproducible experiments and measurable performance metrics. The emphasis will be on developing reliable sensing and data-processing methods that can support practical applications while preserving privacy.

summer: |

Review existing smart parking applications and consider challenges involving sensor accuracy, connectivity, data latency, system reliability, and user experience. Students should also explore methods for validating sensor measurements against observed parking occupancy and designing reproducible experiments. The goal is to develop an understanding of the complete pipeline from deployed radar sensors to a reliable, accessible web application.
