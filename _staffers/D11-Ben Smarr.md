description: |
  Assisted reproductive technology (ART), particularly in vitro fertilization (IVF), involves a series of highly coordinated clinical and laboratory procedures that require precise timing, communication, and collaboration among physicians, nurses, anesthesiology staff, and embryologists. One particularly important stage is oocyte (egg) retrieval, in which mature eggs are collected from a patient's ovaries following ovarian stimulation and preparation. This procedure involves multiple interconnected steps, from patient preparation and anesthesia to follicular aspiration, egg identification, and transfer to the embryology laboratory for processing. Each stage requires coordination across clinical teams, and the timing and documentation of procedural events can provide valuable insight into workflow efficiency, resource utilization, and opportunities for process improvement. As healthcare increasingly incorporates digital tools, data analytics, and process optimization, there is an opportunity to better understand how clinical workflow data can be collected, standardized, and analyzed to identify bottlenecks, improve coordination, and support more consistent and efficient procedures. This domain sits at the intersection of reproductive medicine, human-centered software design, healthcare operations research, and applied data science, with the broader goal of using data-driven insights to improve clinical workflows while maintaining patient safety and quality of care.
